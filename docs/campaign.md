# The Long Trail

Campaign proposal, revised 19 September 2026. A hopeful space western about a herd becoming an alien civilization. Dustlight now implements one persistent automation trailhead operated by an external agent and viewed in the browser. The larger characters, settlements, moving arks, discoveries, and scenes remain fiction for an unbuilt full campaign. Earlier bounded Rust journeys implement additional pieces of the trail; none demonstrates the complete progression below.

## The playable trailhead

The primary playable trailhead is Dustlight. `platonik world` preserves one 24×14 homestead across bounded advances, original-cell policy changes, and placed facility sites. It starts with two supplied courier blueprints and thirty material units across three deposits. Drills, fabricators, assemblers, storehouses, and cranes support repeatable production and supplied construction within finite stock. `/play` recomputes the history and renders it without mutating the agent's local JSON world. The [living-world plan](living-world-plan.md) describes current production and the proposed path toward larger regions.

Several earlier short journeys remain available through the same deterministic Rust interpreter, checker, cost ledger, and replay discipline:

- [The field expedition](field-expedition.md) keeps a collection, records a failed crossing, grows a descendant, freezes one courier-controller pair, and checks it unchanged on four confirmation crossings.
- [The First Answer](first-answer.md) carries one saved habitat through material-dependent construction to a checked local contact ending.
- [Ark control](ark-control.md) makes physical input drive four-bit arithmetic and a remembered service choice; [port commitments](port-commitments.md) checks two finite handoffs through missing and delayed replies.
- [The Bloom](bloom.md) grows and tests two bounded variants. The [one-lane exchange](composition-v5-gate.md) then connects a generated winner to a request, physical delivery, custody acknowledgment, and service inside one continuous world.

Those earlier slices are not folded into the Dustlight save. Completing one does not unlock another; there is no star map, moving ark, independent settlement network, or six-chapter ending. Dustlight supplies continuity for its routes, foundry, and production chain, while the field expedition resets declared trials and the later habitat journeys preserve their own fixed physical worlds. Connecting these capabilities into one campaign remains future work.

The [observatory](observatory.md) displays bounded browser experiments and recorded Rust evidence. The sections below describe the larger journey these working pieces are intended to earn.

## One companion, a distant light

You arrive at a weathered homestead on the edge of settled space. You have a small laboratory, a flickering beacon, and an instrument that can search the space of possible life. Somewhere far beyond the charted routes, another light is answering.

Your first discovery is a strange little pattern that can carry a spark. Give it a body, a name, and a place to live. Help it find its way through the dust. Before you understand what the distant signal means, you have someone to make the journey with.

**Follow the Far Beacon. Grow the civilization that can reach it.**

The player is a **frontier engineer of living systems**: part naturalist, part trail guide, part builder. Travel between alien habitats, discover forms that can flourish there, gather a remarkable herd, and grow a home that can travel with it. Your shipboard agent helps turn “what if?” into something you can watch. The space-western warmth stays, while engineering names the skill you develop: making systems that work together under real constraints.

In this fiction, Platonic space is a wilderness of possible forms. Searching it finds candidate patterns; a body and habitat let a pattern express itself. The star map connects places to explore, while the laboratory explores possible organisms. They are related layers, not interchangeable maps. This is a story premise inspired by the [research questions](research.md), not a claim that software has confirmed a realm of pre-existing living minds.

## One medium through the whole journey

The herd grows inside a proposed [shared programmable medium](autoverse.md). A companion is a body, local rules, and state. Connecting it to a relay, memory, controller, or builder changes what the assembly can do; it does not switch the player into an unrelated puzzle game. Transport, signal, memory, and construction modules have explicit interfaces, versions, and costs.

The verbs remain **inspect, change, connect, run, compare, keep, and reuse**. Early transport policies become an ark's circulation. A remembered report becomes part of a service controller. The controller helps a port keep its promises and later schedules construction. These dependencies must occur in the executable design, not only in the narrator's explanation.

The agent supplies readable reference components and helps build alternatives. Players choose missions, architecture, and questions; hand-wiring a CPU is optional. Every chapter has a finite scenario manifest with resource limits and an independently checked witness of feasibility. Before release, calibrate allowances to admit more than one viable architecture. The contracts below specify what must be measured; their numerical limits are not production promises. The [validation plan](design-validation.md) tests whether the transitions work technically and whether players find them worth pursuing.

## A spark in the dust

The first ambition is small: keep the homestead's beacon alight. Moth takes the short road. Moss tries the unfamiliar one. When a passage closes, a habit that seemed inefficient may bring the light home.

You change a rule, grow a descendant, and compare its journey with its parent's. The original stays with you. Success restores a local connection and reveals the next destination; failure leaves a readable problem and another idea to try. This is the proposed [first beacon expedition](engine.md#first-playable-acceptance).

The distant pulse becomes the campaign's question: **who keeps that light going?** Its existence is authored story content. Whether your creature succeeds in a particular rescue comes from the simulation.

- **Wish and lever:** “Bring the light home when the easy road closes.” Change a local rule or connect a complementary courier policy.
- **Inspectable failure:** a trace shows the precise blocked move, wasted loop, or late delivery that lets the beacon reach zero.
- **Success contract:** complete every declared case within its limits, meet the delivery target, and retain positive beacon charge after every scheduled drain, including the last. The resource ledger must balance.
- **Allowance and alternatives:** the manifest fixes cells, code, memory, spring supply, initial charge, ticks, and execution fuel. A detour policy, a mixed colony, or another admitted local design can qualify; Moth and Moss are suggestions.
- **What travels onward:** keep the actual policy, lineage, body, and replay. The next migration begins with this working transport service and its known limitations.

## The long trail

One good courier cannot cross every landscape. A floodplain needs a different habit from a field of closing passages. You start discovering companions whose abilities complement one another and choose a small expedition herd: who scouts, who carries, who keeps a route usable?

Now the new action is **prepare a migration**. Pick a route, assemble a roster, pack a finite reserve, and adapt to a sequence of habitats. A creature overlooked at home can become the reason the herd gets through. New body and sensing rules arrive only through explicit, separately validated habitat modules.

Discovery needs recognizable families and observable habits, alongside room for surprise. An unfamiliar shape is an invitation to try something; rarity comes from finding a useful behavior or unusual combination under stated tests. Random colors and a new hash alone cannot carry the collection game.

At the next camp, your field guide records a new lineage and a new stretch of trail. The signal has more structure than anyone expected.

A later scene worth earning: the herd returns to a troublesome valley, and a familiar traveler takes a turn its fresh descendants miss. You recognize an old companion through something it does. The notebook can then investigate [what carries that history](research.md#the-remembered-trail), without inventing a memory for the sake of the story.

- **Wish and lever:** “Let the others know the route is safe, even if contact breaks.” Connect transport to a bounded signal relay and a retained report. A reference latch is available to inspect and adapt.
- **Inspectable failure:** an old report is mistaken for a new one, a signal arrives too late, or memory disappears while a messenger is away. The trace shows input, stored identifier, output, and delay.
- **Success contract:** move the declared cargo through the habitat sequence, relay the specified report identifier, retain it across an admitted gap in contact, and return its matching acknowledgment within the deadline. The checker rejects a stale acknowledgment.
- **Allowance and alternatives:** pack one finite reserve for the sequence. The manifest additionally caps ports, queued messages, retained bytes, link reach, and transmission work. Stationary relays or traveling messengers can use the same declared message interface; neither receives free distance or global knowledge.
- **What travels onward:** the migration's relay and report memory become reusable inputs to the ark. The first camp that answers your companion's signal is earned by the actual exchange.

## A herd that carries home

Longer journeys expose a limit: every expedition still depends on supplies from the homestead. To go farther, the herd needs to carry a working habitat with it.

You begin building with its abilities. Courier colonies form circulation routes. Other specialists buffer supplies, maintain passages, or relay local signals. Design their connections, find the bottleneck, and watch the whole arrangement keep working through a disruption. A successful assembly becomes a reusable component with its real costs preserved.

This is the first great change of scale: **the home you built becomes a living ark**. Its first departure is a chapter event earned by a checked autonomous voyage, not by reaching an arbitrary creature count. The settlers inside are still inspectable. The routes you once laid out by hand are running beneath your feet.

An intended future scene: you stand in a garden aboard the ark while a descendant of your first courier carries light through its branches. The skyline moves. Home is leaving the planet.

The ark also changes what can live along the trail. A small colony keeps a waystation's passage open; later, an unfamiliar caravan makes it through because that work continues. Your first companions can become known for a place they help sustain. Initial assembly and upkeep must be accounted for, and the maintained passage and visitors' crossing must occur in the simulation. Automating construction comes later. This is how the herd begins making room for a civilization.

- **Wish and lever:** “Let home look after itself while we travel.” Use the earlier relays and memory to connect logic, arithmetic, and programmable service control to the existing supply routes.
- **Inspectable failure:** two destinations request the same reserve, a counter overflows, or a controller keeps following a stale plan after a local input changes. Inspect the controller's inputs and decisions beside the delayed deliveries.
- **Success contract:** demonstrate the declared arithmetic interface, including carry, on its complete bounded input set; then run two supplied service plans through the same admitted control interface. During each voyage case, keep every required service within its published bounds and fulfill its delivery commitments without external instructions.
- **Allowance and alternatives:** code, live nodes, state, ports, activation work, and finite supplies share one voyage budget. A table-driven controller, composed state machines, or a processor can qualify. Reference logic and arithmetic blueprints expand into charged parts. Building a full stored-program computer is an optional [Autoverse achievement](autoverse.md#what-counts-as-a-computer), not a required hand-wiring lesson.
- **What travels onward:** this complete service assembly becomes a port's working body. The descendant carrying light through the moving garden must be visible in its real transport trace.

The [recorded ark experiment](https://platonik.space/lab/ark) tests the arithmetic-to-control dependency inside a fixed habitat: a physical number becomes a calculated decision that Keeper remembers through an outage. Reserve and Staggered plans use the same selector interface. It is one calculation with a finite service commitment, not the moving garden described here. Repeated regulation and integration with the construction journey remain part of earning this complete chapter.

## The free ports

Other caravans have found different ways to live. Some travel in compact, resilient bodies; others depend on networks of specialists. No one has a complete route to the Far Beacon.

The new action is **connect independent homes**. Establish waystations, arrange exchanges, and develop signals that work between unfamiliar bodies. Design for delayed deliveries and interrupted contact. A settlement earns its place in the network by maintaining its declared needs and commitments when you stop directing individual couriers.

This gives the [shared economy](economy.md) a place in the story. A port needs help keeping a passage open. Another offers a commission for a colony that can travel light. A creature you developed for a private problem finds a role across the frontier. Players pay for useful work and publish reusable discoveries; the inhabitants themselves are not auction items.

The campaign must also supply local settlements and contracts so it remains playable without an active multiplayer market. Hosted player commissions are an optional outer layer, not a requirement to finish the story.

The signal now sounds less like a single voice. Your own network is beginning to produce something similar.

- **Wish and lever:** “Keep a promise to another home without knowing everything it knows.” Connect independently controlled habitats through a bounded delivery-and-acknowledgment protocol.
- **Inspectable failure:** a lost reply triggers a duplicate request; two ports each believe the other still holds a parcel. Compare message identifiers and both custody ledgers to find the disagreement.
- **Success contract:** fulfill all declared finite commitments under the allowed delays, message losses, and duplicate deliveries. Each parcel has one accountable location; duplicate messages cannot create a second delivery credit. Required habitat services must continue throughout the exchange.
- **Allowance and alternatives:** the manifest caps link capacity, in-flight messages, retries, retained identifiers, cargo, and total work. Choose a hub, a chain, or a more distributed arrangement that fits those bounds. Local authored ports supply the entire campaign's needs even if the player market is empty.
- **What travels onward:** the same transport and service controllers now procure and deliver construction stock. A neighboring home recognizes a lineage because it actually honored its promise.

The [recorded port experiment](https://platonik.space/lab/ports) isolates two one-shot promises in local neighborhoods of one saved world. Couriers preserve a same-bit spare while retrying replies, and the checker distinguishes accepted custody from acknowledgment and beacon service. Its [evaluation](ports-evaluation.md) does not establish distant independent worlds, repeated sessions, or the market described in this chapter.

## The bloom

Until now, you and your agent have designed the next creature. A later settlement acquires a bounded process that proposes and tests new organisms itself. Your task becomes choosing the questions, habitats, and resource allowances under which those discoveries can grow.

The first implemented slice is the [Bloom habitat](bloom.md) and its [evaluation](bloom-evaluation.md): two builders edit copies of one seed, children run physical trials, and a selector earns a later confirmation from a real depot report. It is one generation with two direction loci, a concrete bridge toward this chapter's larger arc.

This is the singularity moment: **your creations begin creating**. It must be demonstrated by an actual generated candidate, a visible change from its ancestors, and a checked result. A scripted announcement that “intelligence increased” cannot substitute for the new capability. Search, copying, testing, and communication still cost compute; expansion never grants permission for unbounded background spending.

Power becomes tangible: living ships carry other living ships, ports maintain routes without a central dispatcher, and a civilization can prepare an expedition you could not have assembled creature by creature. You still choose its ambitions and constraints. You can still visit the original homestead.

The revealing setback is a departure into unfamiliar conditions where the network's shared assumptions fail. Making more of the same successful lineage does not solve the new coordination problem. The player must discover which capabilities and connections actually matter, using controlled comparisons rather than a compulsory diversity bonus.

- **Wish and lever:** “Build the next waystation, and help find a design that can keep it alive.” First direct a finite blueprint assembler; then connect a separately earned bounded candidate-generation and testing process to it.
- **Inspectable failure:** a wrong connection prevents an assembly from activating, or a candidate passes its familiar test and fails a new admitted condition. The record exposes the blueprint, placements, candidate changes, test inputs, and remaining budget.
- **Success contract:** build the declared component from finite stock and verify its ordinary interface. In the later search gate, the running assembly must generate at least one changed candidate from the declared family, evaluate candidates through the metered harness, and select one that passes the frozen confirmation case after search ends. Scenario admission must establish that this goal is attainable; it does not demand a novel scientific discovery.
- **Allowance and alternatives:** one outer budget covers generation, copying, construction, all candidate execution, failures, and selection. An enumerator, a mutation-and-selection process, or another admitted procedure can qualify. Reference generators expose their operations; there is no free `search` or `evolve` instruction.
- **What travels onward:** preserve both the successful component and the procedure that produced it. Your favorite's descendants now help prepare a destination for travelers who have not arrived yet.

## Across the Quiet

The Far Beacon lies beyond a region where the familiar relay network cannot reach. Routes change, messages arrive late, and the fleet must keep its own habitats going. The final campaign expedition brings together everything the journey taught: useful individuals, compatible groups, dependable infrastructure, and discovery under limited resources.

**Carry a living civilization across the Quiet and establish a lasting two-way connection.** The finale needs a published scenario contract: complete the voyage, maintain the required habitat services throughout it, and deliver and acknowledge the signal under the allowed communication rules. Feasibility, costs, failures, and outcomes remain checkable. A bigger fleet alone is not a win.

Your first companion's lineage can supply a small part of the solution—the same recovery habit, now keeping an ark's light routes open. Make that contribution available and visible without secretly requiring one starter genome. Players who took another path deserve a climax that reflects their own herd.

The authored revelation is that the Far Beacon is sustained by another civilization of many cooperating forms. The pulse was an invitation whose structure you could only understand after learning to build something that could answer. Successful play earns the reply; the particular fleet and rescue that delivered it belong to the player's run. What that reply could sound like — a bounded voice knowing only its own world — is a separate proposal: [a voice on the wire](voices.md).

The final scene returns to a little light moving between two places. You began by helping one creature make that journey. Now you have helped a civilization learn how to keep making it.

- **Wish and lever:** “Take everyone across, and give that distant light a way to answer.” Reuse the herd's transport, remembered reports, service control, port protocol, and admitted construction process in one expedition.
- **Inspectable failure:** a late acknowledgment strands a supply shipment, an unfinished waystation exhausts a reserve, or a locally successful adaptation breaks an interface another habitat depends on. Follow the failure across component boundaries.
- **Success contract:** complete the finite voyage; preserve every specified service invariant throughout it; construct and activate the required continuation after launch; deliver the outbound message and its matching reply through the allowed links before the deadline. Any required adaptation uses the declared bounded candidate process. The independent checker judges the entire trace, including failed work, rather than only the last frame.
- **Allowance and alternatives:** the final manifest fixes one total work allowance, supplies, active-region bounds, memory, message capacity, construction stock, and horizon. Reuse earlier components or compatible replacements. Compact and distributed fleets are both eligible; no starter identity or population count is a hidden win condition.
- **Earned ending:** the actual successful exchange unlocks the authored reply. The game can show which of your lineages carried its messages, sustained its homes, and built its last safe stop.

## The herd must feel like company

Warmth has to live in the rules. Parents remain intact when a new descendant is grown. A favorite can keep its body and home while related patterns take on other roles. Joining a larger assembly does not consume an individual as a crafting ingredient. Failed expeditions lose progress within their declared scenario, not the player's entire collection.

Later habitat systems should give modeled preferences practical consequences: a creature may settle in a suitable niche, rest when its reserve is low, or leave an unsuitable assignment. The player changes the conditions or chooses another specialist; a decorative “happy” label cannot conceal a universal obedience command. These are future simulation rules, not proof of consent or consciousness, and they are not hidden additions to the first courier VM.

Growth should offer choices among useful futures. Keep a nimble traveler, grow a larger descendant, or form a partnership. No mandatory sacrifice, irreversible fusion, body-horror transformation, or reward for torment is needed. A peaceful sanctuary remains a valid place for an old favorite.

The space-western texture comes from camp lights, long journeys, eccentric travelers, rival caravans, and homes built in improbable places. Tension comes from a difficult crossing, an interrupted supply route, a rescue request, or a rival solving a frontier challenge first. Rivalry follows [fair expedition rules](competition.md); it does not raid another player's save. Offline time never harms the herd.

“Civilization” describes the campaign's fiction and later modeled collective capabilities. The notebook continues to report exactly what the programs did. Character dialogue, creature imagery, and an authored first contact do not establish a scientific claim about minds.

## An ending, followed by a frontier

Answering the beacon finishes the authored campaign. It does not reset the herd or solve every possible world. The player can remain at home, lead a new caravan, investigate an unfamiliar form, commission research, or enter a versioned competitive season. New destinations need new constraints and decisions, not only different scenery.

The campaign's real research trail grows alongside its fiction: recovery experiments, body-policy comparisons, communication protocols, compact reusable programs, and eventually bounded search procedures. Useful findings require independent checking. P versus NP remains an optional, carefully specified research direction; no campaign scene grants a mathematical proof or a “percentage of P=NP solved.”

The design takes changes of scale from [Universal Paperclips](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/index2.html), a few mechanically distinct progression destinations from [Factorio: Space Age's design](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-373), and the horizon of exploration from [No Man's Sky](https://www.nomanssky.com/about/). Companion attachment and choosing a complementary roster carry the creature-collection fantasy. The frontier's exchange of specialized work connects that journey to the economy.

Before expanding the engine, test the smallest piece of this promise: does a player remember a companion, understand its new habit, and want to take it to the next camp? The later arc guides that prototype; it does not require building a space simulator, civilization engine, or market first.
